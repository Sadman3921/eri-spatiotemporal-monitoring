Onboard, information-driven environmental monitoring with lightweight field models, informative decision policies, and autonomous surface vehicles.

Abstract

Surface waters can change over hours to days under contamination events such as fuel and oil spills, chemical releases, and harmful algal blooms. This project investigates how autonomous surface vehicles (ASVs) with multimodal sensors can repeatedly collect informative in-situ measurements across lakes, bayous, and rivers while operating under strict onboard computation, energy, sensing, and communication limits.

The project develops a low-rank spatiotemporal field modeling framework, compact information-state updates, lightweight on-device decision policies, and limited-range multi-robot coordination. The goal is to let robots decide where, when, and how to sample so that environmental fields can be estimated accurately without relying on heavy offboard computation.

Method

System Overview

The framework treats environmental monitoring as a robotic information gathering problem. Incoming measurements update a compact information state that summarizes the current field estimate and uncertainty. Candidate sensing locations are scored by expected information gain together with mission costs, and a lightweight policy selects the next action for real-time execution on the ASV.

Low-Rank Field Modeling

A dynamic scalar field such as temperature, dissolved oxygen, chlorophyll, or pollution concentration is represented with a compact set of spatial basis functions:

f(x,t) ≈ h(x)Tat

The latent weights evolve over time, providing a scalable representation of spatial and temporal variation.

Information-State Planning

The information state stores the estimated field weights and their uncertainty. Candidate locations are ranked using uncertainty and approximate mutual information while accounting for travel distance, heading change, arrival time, energy, data age, trend, and safety constraints.

Lightweight Informative Policy

The decision policy is designed for real robot deployment. It consumes compact information-state features, selects among a small set of informative candidate locations, and is intended to be compressed and quantized for resource-constrained onboard execution.

Research Objectives

RO1

Modeling & Coordination

Develop efficient spatiotemporal field models and decentralized multi-robot coordination with limited communication.

RO2

Lightweight Decision-Making

Develop deployable information-state–driven policies that can make safe and efficient sampling decisions in real time.

RO3

Experimental Validation

Validate the framework through theoretical analysis, large-scale simulation, and real-world field experiments.

Field Experiments

Preliminary deployments use a BlueBoat autonomous surface vehicle equipped with a YSI EXO multiparameter sonde for in-situ water quality measurements. Field trials at Pine Island Bayou in Lumberton, Texas provide georeferenced measurements for environmental field visualization, simulation design, sensing-noise characterization, and subsequent algorithm validation.

Research Plan

Task T1: Modeling and Coordination

Construct compact spatiotemporal field models, update the information state online, generate informative candidate locations, and coordinate multiple ASVs under limited-range communication.

Task T2: Lightweight Policy Learning

Train a compact policy over information-state features using imitation learning, domain randomization, and reinforcement-learning fine-tuning for fast onboard action selection.

Task T3: Analysis and Experimental Validation

Evaluate theoretical properties, simulation performance, scalability, and real-world feasibility through field deployments and multi-robot experiments.

Evaluation

Planned metrics include field-estimation accuracy, information gain, time-to-hotspot, duplication rate, cycle latency, memory/runtime scaling, energy efficiency, safety, and adaptation performance.
